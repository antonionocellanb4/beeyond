Technology

Four ideas
do all the work.

Both machines separate solids from water without a filter medium. Everything else — the low speed, the small motor, the litres of rinse water — follows from that one decision.

01

No filter medium

The gap between moving parts does the filtering. There is no cloth or mesh to blind.

02

Self-cleaning geometry

Neighbouring parts move against each other every revolution and wipe the slot open.

03

Low-speed mechanics

Slow rotation means a wear-free structure, no vibration, no noise, no foundation.

04

Energy and water

0.1 to 8.2 kW installed. Rinse water measured in litres per hour, not cubic metres.

Built to be
boring in service.

A dewatering machine earns its money on the days nobody looks at it. Every choice below exists to remove a reason for someone to open the lid: no medium to replace, no spray bar to unblock, no gearbox running fast enough to care about.

In detail

Four principles,
one at a time.

01

Filtration without a medium

The filtering surface is the calibrated gap between stacked rings or between rotating oval discs. Solids bridge across it, water passes through it.

  • Nothing to blind, so capacity does not fade between cleans
  • Handles fat, hair and fibre that blind wedge wire
  • Sludge from 2 000 to 50 000 mg/L on the same machine
02

Self-cleaning geometry

On the screw press the free rings ride with the screw; on the separator the oval discs turn in sync. Either way the two surfaces forming the slot never stop moving relative to each other.

  • No backwash pump, no clean-water line, no nozzles
  • Rinse water from 24 L/h on the smallest press
  • No filter media in the spares list, ever
03

Low-speed mechanics

Nothing spins fast. That removes the vibration, the noise, the dynamic foundation and most of the wear that makes a centrifuge a maintenance item.

  • Wear-free structure, installs on a flat floor
  • Quiet enough to sit indoors next to people
  • Fully automatic: unattended nights and weekends
04

Energy, water and cake

Small motors, litres of rinse water, and a cake dry enough to shovel. The running cost is the argument for replacing a belt press or a decanter.

  • 0.1 kW on the smallest screen, 8.2 kW on the largest press
  • Cake at 20% dry solids and above
  • Replaces belt press, decanter centrifuge or filter press
